Biography & Legacy

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam was an eminent aerospace scientist who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Born and raised in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, he studied physics and aerospace engineering, eventually spending over four decades as a scientist and science administrator, mainly at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO).

He was intimately involved in India's civilian space programme and military missile development efforts. He thus came to be known as the "Missile Man" for his pioneering work on the development of ballistic missile technology and launch vehicle systems, playing a pivotal organizational and technical role in landmark space projects.

Beyond his scientific achievements, Kalam was deeply admired for his humility, simple lifestyle, and passion for inspiring young minds. After completing his presidential term, he returned to his civilian life of writing, teaching, and public service, traveling across educational institutions to motivate students to contribute towards nation-building.

Key Achievements & Timeline

  1. 1960: Graduated from Madras Institute of Technology and joined DRDO as a senior scientific assistant.
  2. 1969: Transferred to ISRO where he served as the project director of India's first Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III).
  3. 1998: Served as the Chief Scientific Adviser to the Prime Minister and played a key organizational role in aerospace testing.
  4. 1997: Honoured with the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award, for his contributions to scientific research.
  5. 2002–2007: Served as the 11th President, earning the affectionate title of the "People's President".
